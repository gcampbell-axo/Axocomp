For injured workers

Hurt at work? Here's how workers' comp works.

Workers' comp can feel confusing and slow. This guide explains the Tennessee process in plain language so you know what to expect and who to call.

What is workers' compensation?

It's insurance your employer carries for work injuries. If you're hurt on the job, it pays for your medical care and part of your lost wages while you recover.

Who pays for my care?

It's a no-fault system. In most cases it doesn't matter who caused the accident, and you don't pay for care from your authorized doctor on an accepted claim.

  1. 1

    Report the injury

    Right away

    Tell your supervisor or the person named on your workplace's workers' comp poster as soon as possible, even if the injury seems minor. Ask for a copy of the injury report.

  2. 2

    Choose your doctor from a panel

    Form C-42

    In Tennessee, your employer gives you a list of at least three doctors, called a panel. You pick one by signing Form C-42. That doctor becomes your authorized treating physician (ATP) and directs your care.

    Keep a copy of your signed form. In an emergency, get care first. The panel comes after you're stable.

  3. 3

    Get evaluated and referred

    ATP referral

    If your injury involves your nerves, brain or spine, your ATP may refer you to a neurologist like Axocomp, or Axocomp may be on your panel. Specialist visits and tests usually need the insurance adjuster's approval first.

  4. 4

    Follow your work status

    Every visit

    After each visit your doctor sets your work status: full duty, light duty with restrictions, or off work. If your employer offers light duty within your restrictions, you're expected to take it. Turning it down can stop your wage benefits.

  5. 5

    Wage benefits if you're off work

    After 7 days

    If your ATP takes you off work, temporary disability benefits usually pay about two-thirds of your average weekly wage. They start on day 8. If you're off 14 days or more, you're paid back to day 1. Your employer's insurance company pays these benefits, not the state.

  6. 6

    Reach MMI and a rating

    End of treatment

    Maximum medical improvement (MMI) means you've reached the point where your doctor doesn't expect further improvement. Your doctor then decides whether you have a lasting impairment and gives it a rating, which is used to figure out any permanent disability benefit.

Common frustrations, answered

Why is everything taking so long?

Most visits and tests need the adjuster's approval before they can be scheduled. We send authorization requests quickly, follow up, and tell you when we're waiting on approval.

Why didn't I get paid for my first week off?

Tennessee doesn't pay wage benefits for the first 7 days unless you end up off work 14 days or more. Check whether your employer lets you use sick or vacation time.

Can I see my own doctor?

Your care is directed by the ATP you picked from the panel. You can get a second opinion on your own, but you may have to pay for it. Ask the adjuster before you see anyone outside the claim.

Do I get paid for driving to appointments?

You can ask for mileage reimbursement when the trip is more than 15 miles one way. Keep a log of your trips. Time at appointments usually isn't paid unless your employer's policy covers it.

Words you'll hear

Adjuster
The insurance company employee who manages your claim and approves care and payments.
Panel (C-42)
The list of at least three doctors from which you choose your treating physician.
ATP
Authorized treating physician: the doctor you chose from the panel, who directs your care.
Nurse case manager
A nurse the insurer may assign to coordinate your care and appointments.
Light duty
Modified work within your restrictions while you recover.
TTD
Temporary total disability: wage benefits while your doctor has you off work.
MMI
Maximum medical improvement: the point where more recovery isn't expected.
Impairment rating
A percentage your doctor assigns at MMI if the injury left a lasting effect.

Having trouble with your claim?

The Tennessee Bureau of Workers' Compensation has free ombudsmen who answer questions and help when benefits or care are delayed or denied. If you were hurt in a car or other accident and have an attorney, call your attorney first.

Diagnosis

Left C6 radiculopathy

Job description

Full-time warehouse associate. Lifts and carries cartons of 20 to 60 lb throughout the shift, with frequent reaching, overhead stacking and pallet jack use.

History of injury

This is a 41-year-old right-handed male warehouse associate presenting for evaluation of a work-related injury sustained on 01/12/2027. While lifting a 60 lb carton from a low shelf to a pallet, he experienced sudden sharp left-sided neck pain radiating into the left shoulder, lateral forearm, thumb and index finger. He reported the injury to his supervisor the same day. He continues to report numbness in the left thumb and index finger and weakness when lifting with the left arm. Denies prior neck injury, neck treatment, upper extremity symptoms or workers' compensation claims. Non-smoker.

Frequency of symptoms: Daily, with intermittent numbness and weakness exacerbated by lifting with the left arm.

Duration of symptoms: Neck and arm pain lasting several hours daily; numbness lasting 30–60 minutes per episode.

Records reviewed

01/12/2027 Employer first report of injury: Left neck and arm pain while lifting a 60 lb carton from a low shelf. Injury reported to supervisor the same day.

01/12/2027 Urgent care evaluation: Neck pain radiating into the left arm with tingling in the left thumb. Reduced cervical ROM; strength not formally assessed. Diagnosed with cervical strain. Ibuprofen and light duty prescribed. Employer physician panel provided.

01/12/2027 Cervical spine X-rays: Normal alignment without fracture. Mild C5-C6 disc space narrowing.

01/13/2027 Form C-42: Patient selected Axocomp Neurology as authorized treating physician from the employer panel.

01/15/2027 Axocomp initial evaluation: Left biceps and wrist extensor weakness (4/5), diminished left biceps reflex and decreased sensation in the left thumb and index finger, documented as consistent with left C6 radiculopathy. Causation opinion documented. Modified duty assigned. Cervical MRI and EMG/NCS ordered.

02/03/2027 MRI cervical spine: Left C5-C6 disc protrusion with moderate left foraminal narrowing contacting the exiting left C6 nerve root. Mild C5-C6 disc degeneration. No spinal cord compression.

Diagnostic testing performed at Axocomp

02/09/2027 EMG/NCS LUE: Normal sensory and motor nerve conduction studies. Needle EMG demonstrated active denervation with fibrillations, positive sharp waves and reduced recruitment in the left biceps, brachioradialis, pronator teres and left C6 paraspinal muscles. Impression: Electrodiagnostic evidence of left C6 radiculopathy without evidence of carpal tunnel syndrome or other nerve entrapment.

02/09/2027 MRI cervical spine image review: Images from 02/03/2027 personally reviewed. Left C5-C6 disc protrusion with left foraminal narrowing, documented as correlating with clinical and EMG findings. Mild C5-C6 disc degeneration without prior symptoms.

Current treatment

Ibuprofen as needed and light duty.

Failed treatment

Rest, ibuprofen and light duty have not resolved the left thumb and index finger numbness or left arm weakness.

Examination

Mental status and cranial nerves: Normal. Motor: Left biceps and wrist extensors 4/5; otherwise 5/5. Reflexes: Left biceps and brachioradialis 1+; otherwise 2+ and symmetric. Sensation: Decreased pinprick over the left thumb and index finger. Cervical spine: Reduced left rotation; Spurling's test positive on the left, reproducing left arm pain. Effort: Consistent, without non-physiologic findings.

Causation

Considering all causes, it is my opinion, to a reasonable degree of medical certainty (more likely than not, as opposed to speculation or possibility), that the January 12, 2027 lifting incident contributed more than fifty percent (50%) in causing Mr. Sample's left C6 radiculopathy and his need for medical treatment. This opinion is based on: a specific incident identifiable by time and place; immediate onset of symptoms matching the C6 nerve root; no prior neck or arm symptoms or treatment; and objective EMG and MRI findings consistent with the mechanism of injury. The mild degenerative change at C5-C6 was present without symptoms before the incident and does not account for the acute radiculopathy. Today's EMG and MRI findings confirm the causation opinion given at the initial visit on January 15, 2027.

Work status

Modified duty, effective today until the next visit. No lifting over 15 lb with the left arm. No overhead work. No repetitive pushing or pulling. May use the right arm without restriction.

Treatment plan and expected timeline

  • Physical therapy, 2 to 3 times a week for 6 weeks (authorization requested)
  • Short course of oral steroid, then a nerve pain medication as needed
  • If not improving at follow-up: cervical epidural steroid injection referral
  • If weakness progresses: spine surgery consultation
  • Follow-up visit in 4 weeks with repeat strength testing
  • As authorized treating physician, Axocomp will submit and coordinate these referrals with the adjuster

Maximum medical improvement and impairment

Not at MMI. MMI is expected in about 8 to 12 weeks if the patient improves with conservative care. At MMI, a permanent impairment rating will be assigned using the AMA Guides, 6th Edition, and reported on Tennessee Form C-30A.
